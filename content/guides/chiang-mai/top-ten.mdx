title: Top 10 Things to Experience
description: Ten experiences that tell the story of Chiang Mai.
---

Chiang Mai runs at a different speed. Mornings belong to the mountain -
Doi Suthep before the tour buses, mist still on the road - afternoons to
cafés, and evenings to markets where dinner costs less than your flat
white did. It's a city you settle into rather than conquer, and these
ten are how it tells its story. They're numbered to keep count, not to
rank - every one of these earns its place.

## 1. Doi Suthep at sunrise

The golden chedi above the city, before the tour buses arrive. Ride up
the mountain road early, climb the naga staircase, and watch the whole
valley wake up below you. This is Chiang Mai's morning at its purest -
mist, gold, and temple bells.

## 2. A day with the elephants + bamboo rafting

The reason many people come north at all - a sanctuary day feeding and
walking with rescued elephants, ending with a waterfall swim and a slow
bamboo raft down the river. Choose a sanctuary (no riding) and it
becomes the day you talk about for years.

## 3. Old City temple walk

Chiang Mai has over 300 temples; walk between these four and you've
seen its whole range. Wat Phra Singh - Lanna gold at its brightest.
Wat Chedi Luang - a massive ruined brick tower at the old city's
heart. The Black Temple, Wat Inthakhin Sadue Mueang - dark teak edged
in gold, hiding in plain sight by the city pillar. And ten minutes
south of the moat, Wat Sri Suphan, the Silver Temple - an entire hall
in hammered silver that glows violet under the evening lights.

## 4. Khao soi, done properly

Chiang Mai's signature dish - egg noodles in golden curry broth,
crispy noodles on top, pickled mustard greens on the side. The
institutions serve it until the pot runs out, so go for lunch, not
dinner. One bowl in and you'll understand why northerners are smug
about their food.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Khao+Soi+Khun+Yai+Chiang+Mai"

## 5. Feed the carp at Chom Cafe

A café built like a rainforest ravine - waterfalls, mist drifting
through hanging gardens, and ponds of koi carp that swarm over for
feeding time. It sounds like a gimmick; it feels like a fairytale.
Come for the spectacle, stay for a surprisingly good lunch.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Chom+Cafe+and+Restaurant+Chiang+Mai"
  name="Chom Cafe & Restaurant"

## 6. Brunch at the Coconut Market

Tables scattered through a coconut grove, footbridges over the water,
and stalls of northern food, fruit shakes and grilled everything -
the Coconut Market is Chiang Mai's prettiest morning out. Weekends
only get busier, so go early, order from three different stalls, and
find a table under the palms.

<PlaceCard

  name="Coconut Market"

## 7. One Nimman and the night market

Nimman's landmark corner - a brick courtyard of design shops and
restaurants that turns into a night market after dark, craft stalls
and live music under festoon lights. Start with a sunset coffee, browse
the stalls, and let the evening decide dinner.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=One+Nimman+Chiang+Mai"
  name="One Nimman"
/>

## 8. Sunday Walking Street

Every Sunday evening, Ratchadamnoen Road closes to traffic and fills
end-to-end with craft stalls, street food courts in temple yards, and
buskers under the lanterns. Go around 5pm before the crush, eat dinner
stall by stall, and haggle gently - prices are already fair.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sunday+Walking+Street+Ratchadamnoen+Chiang+Mai"
  name="Sunday Walking Street"
/>

## 9. Doi Inthanon and the twin pagodas

Thailand's highest mountain, cool air, cloud forest, and the two
pagodas standing over a sea of hills - the view from our guide's cover
photo. Rent a car or join a day tour, hit the summit and the Wachirathan
waterfall, and pack a jumper: it's genuinely cold up there.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Doi+Inthanon+Twin+Pagodas"

## 10. An evening on the Ping River

End slow: a riverside table as the lights come on, live acoustic music,
northern food and cold Chang. The riverside stretch is where Chiang Mai
exhales after dark - no rooftops, no queues, just the water and the
night settling in.
