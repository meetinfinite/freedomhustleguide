title: Top 10 Things to Experience

Bangkok always felt like more than one city to us. So big, so layered,
so full of different rhythms that it sometimes feels like several
cities living inside one. Maybe that's why Bangkok stays in your memory
differently - not as one place, but as hundreds of small scenes you
keep replaying years later. Bangkok is never one city.

That's what this Top Ten is for - the experiences that let you feel
each of those layers. Whether you're here for a long weekend or a few
months, cover these and you won't just have seen Bangkok - you'll
understand just how many layers it has. They're numbered to keep
count, not to rank - every one of these earns its place.

## 1. Temple morning: the Grand Palace + Wat Pho

Do them together, early, in one morning - they're neighbours. The Grand
Palace is Bangkok at its most golden; Wat Pho, ten minutes on foot, is
where the city goes quiet around a 46-metre reclining Buddha. Cover your
shoulders and knees, and go at opening time - by 10am you're sharing it
with every tour bus in Thailand.

## 2. Rooftop night at Tichuca

Sixty-something floors up, a glowing jellyfish tree of a bar, and the
whole city grid burning below you. Bangkok's skyline is one of its
layers you have to see from above at least once. Go on a weeknight just
before sunset, dress smart-casual, and expect a queue on weekends.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tichuca+Rooftop+Bar+Bangkok"
  name="Tichuca Rooftop Bar"
/>

## 3. Wat Arun at sunset

Save Wat Arun for the end of a day. Cross the river on the 5-baht ferry
in the late afternoon, walk the spire while the light turns gold, then
head back across for drinks at the Eagle Nest rooftop - the terrace
faces Wat Arun straight on, and watching it light up over a cold drink
is one of Bangkok's great evenings. It's the photo everyone knows - and
it's better in person.

## 4. Muay Thai fight night

Nothing on a screen prepares you for ringside. The live drums, the
crowd betting in hand signals, the sound a shin makes at full speed -
this is Bangkok's intensity distilled into one evening. Buy tickets at
the door and sit ringside; skip the package-tour markups.

## 5. Chinatown after dark

Bangkok's oldest layer at its loudest - woks on fire down Yaowarat
Road, queues that tell you where to eat, neon signs stacked five deep.
Come hungry, eat standing up, and follow the locals rather than a list.
Finish with dessert at one of the hip spots hiding in the sois.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yaowarat+Road+Bangkok"

## 6. A day at ICONSIAM

Bangkok does malls like nowhere else on earth, and ICONSIAM is the
showpiece - an indoor floating market on the ground floor, a river
terrace out front, and air-conditioning that earns its keep in April.
Pair it with a Chao Phraya boat ride; the free shuttle from Sathorn
pier is half the fun.

## 7. Get lost in Chatuchak Weekend Market

Fifteen thousand stalls, and somehow you still find things you've never
seen before - vintage denim next to fighting-fish tanks next to the
best coconut ice cream in the city. Go Saturday or Sunday morning,
surrender the map, and give it three hours minimum.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Chatuchak+Weekend+Market+Bangkok"
  name="Chatuchak Weekend Market"

## 8. Night at the Train Market (Talad Rot Fai Srinakarin)

The night market before night markets were content - sprawling vintage
warehouses on the city's edge, classic cars and barber shops between
the food stalls, neon everywhere, and a crowd that's almost entirely
Thai. It takes effort to get to, which is exactly why it still feels
real. Friday to Sunday evenings only.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Talad+Rot+Fai+Srinakarin+Train+Market"
  name="Train Market Srinakarin (Talad Rot Fai)"

## 9. Khao San Road - one night, not one month

The world's most famous backpacker street deserves exactly one evening:
buckets, live bands, pad thai flipped beside fire jugglers, travellers
from everywhere on their first night in Asia. It's loud, it's chaotic,
and it's part of Bangkok's story - visit it, enjoy it, and sleep
somewhere else.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Khao+San+Road+Bangkok"

## 10. A Thonburi afternoon: canal boat + coffee with the Big Buddha

Twenty minutes from the skyscrapers, Bangkok becomes a water city again.
Take a long-tail boat through the Thonburi canals - stilt houses,
temples on the banks, kids waving from porches - then finish with
riverside coffee at Hidden Bangkok Bistro (find it in our Cafés
section), looking straight at the 69-metre Big Buddha across the water.
Old Bangkok, in one afternoon.
