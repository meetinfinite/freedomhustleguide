title: Top 10 Things to Experience
description: Ten experiences that tell the story of Bangkok.

Bangkok always felt like more than one city to us. So big, so layered,
so full of different rhythms that it sometimes feels like several
cities living inside one. Maybe that's why Bangkok stays in your memory
differently - not as one place, but as hundreds of small scenes you
keep replaying years later. Bangkok is never one city.

That's what this Top Ten is for - the experiences that let you feel
each of those layers. Whether you're here for a long weekend or a few
months, cover these and you won't just have seen Bangkok - you'll
understand just how many layers it has.

## Muay Thai fight night

Nothing on a screen prepares you for ringside. The live drums, the
crowd betting in hand signals, the sound a shin makes at full speed -
this is Bangkok's intensity distilled into one evening. Buy tickets at
the door and sit ringside; skip the package-tour markups.

## Temple morning: the Grand Palace + Wat Pho

Do them together, early, in one morning - they're neighbours. The Grand
Palace is Bangkok at its most golden; Wat Pho, ten minutes on foot, is
where the city goes quiet around a 46-metre reclining Buddha. Cover your
shoulders and knees, and go at opening time - by 10am you're sharing it
with every tour bus in Thailand.

## Wat Arun at sunset

Save Wat Arun for the end of a day. Cross the river on the 5-baht ferry
in the late afternoon, walk the spire while the light turns gold, then
watch it glow from a riverside bar on the opposite bank. It's the
Bangkok photo everyone knows - and it's better in person.

## Rooftop night at Tichuca

Sixty-something floors up, a glowing jellyfish tree of a bar, and the
whole city grid burning below you. Bangkok's skyline is one of its
layers you have to see from above once. Go on a weeknight just before
sunset, dress smart-casual, and expect a queue on weekends.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tichuca+Rooftop+Bar+Bangkok"
  name="Tichuca Rooftop Bar"
/>

## A Yaowarat street-food evening

Chinatown after dark is Bangkok's oldest layer at its loudest - woks on
fire, queues that tell you where to eat, neon signs stacked five deep.
Come hungry, eat standing up, and follow the locals rather than a list.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Yaowarat+Road+Bangkok"
  name="Yaowarat Road (Chinatown)"

## A day at ICONSIAM

Bangkok does malls like nowhere else on earth, and ICONSIAM is the
showpiece - an indoor floating market on the ground floor, a river
terrace out front, and air-conditioning that earns its keep in April.
Pair it with a Chao Phraya boat ride; the free shuttle from Sathorn
pier is half the fun.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=ICONSIAM+Bangkok"

## Learn to cook the food you've been eating

A morning market tour, then four dishes you'll actually make again.
After a cooking class, every menu in Thailand reads differently - and
your pad kra pao at home stops being an apology. One of the best
things we've spent money on in Bangkok.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Silom+Thai+Cooking+School+Bangkok"
  name="Silom Thai Cooking School"
/>

## JJ Green Market on a Saturday night

The night market where the crowd is actually local - vintage stalls,
food trucks, craft beer, live music, no tour groups. This is the layer
of Bangkok most visitors never find, a short walk from MRT Kamphaeng
Phet.

## Maeklong Railway Market

A market built on active train tracks, an hour out of the city. When
the train comes through, vendors fold their awnings back centimetres
from the produce - and then unfold them like nothing happened. Sounds
gimmicky; genuinely one of the coolest 30 minutes near Bangkok.

<PlaceCard
  url="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Maeklong+Railway+Market"
  name="Maeklong Railway Market"
/>

## A day among the ruins of Ayutthaya

The old capital, an hour north - Buddha heads wrapped in tree roots,
brick prangs against big skies, and the best way to see it is by
bicycle between the temples. Take the train up for pocket change or a
minivan tour if you'd rather not think. Go early; the heat is real.
