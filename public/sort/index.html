Pick where your footage will live

Check your size first. On iPhone: Settings → your name → iCloud → Photos. Buy at least double that, so you have room for next year.

My pick

A portable SSD

Small, fast and tough, with no moving parts inside. If you film a lot or want to edit straight from the drive, get this.

  • Best for: lots of video, creators, anyone who travels with it
  • Size: 1 TB for most people, 2 TB if you shoot 4K
  • Costs the most per gigabyte
SanDisk Extreme PRO SSD, 1 TB SanDisk Extreme SSD, 2 TB
Most space for the money

A portable hard drive

The older kind, with a spinning disk inside. You get far more space for the price, but it's slower and it doesn't like being bumped while it's working.

  • Best for: a huge library on a budget, or a second backup copy
  • Size: 1 TB to 5 TB
  • Keep it flat and still while it copies
Seagate Portable, 1 TB Seagate One Touch, 5 TB
Cheapest start

A flash drive

The little stick. It costs the least and it's enough for a first try or a smaller library. There are two kinds, so check which one you're buying.

  • Plugs into your phone (USB-C): you copy photos straight from the phone, no computer needed for that part
  • Plugs into your computer (USB-A): works like a tiny hard drive, with up to 1 TB of space
  • Slower than an SSD with long videos, and easy to lose
  • Don't make it your only copy forever
Lexar Dual Drive, 128 GB (phone + computer) SanDisk Ultra Flair, 1 TB (computer)

Want the drive to work on both Mac and Windows? It needs the exFAT format. Most new drives come that way.

The setup

1

Get your footage off your phone

Always export the full-quality originals. If you want to post on social media or edit your footage with Claude later, you need the highest resolution you have. You can always make smaller copies, but you can't add quality back. Pick your setup:

This is how I do it. A few clicks per batch, and you stay in control of what gets copied.

  1. Make a folder on your drive called To sort.
  2. Open Photos on the Mac. Click the first photo of a batch, scroll down, hold Shift and click the last one.
  3. Look at the top of the window. It says how many items are selected. Write that number down.
  4. Choose File → Export → Export Unmodified Original. Not "Export Photos", which shrinks them.
  5. Set File Name: Use File Name and Subfolder Format: Moment Name. This puts the date and place in each folder name, which Claude reads later.
  6. Save into To sort and wait until the little progress circle at the top of Photos is gone.

Batch size: start with a few hundred to learn it, then 1,000 to 2,000 at a time. For me about 500 arrive per hour.

If you don't want to select batches by hand. A free tool called icloudpd (iCloud Photos Downloader) downloads your iCloud library by itself, and Claude can install and run it for you. I'd still do it in batches, even when it's automatic. Smaller batches are easier to check, and if something stops halfway you lose less.

iPhone + Windows PC, photos in iCloud.

  1. Make a folder on your drive called To sort.
  2. Install iCloud for Windows from the Microsoft Store. Sign in with your Apple account and tick Photos.
  3. Open File Explorer and click iCloud Photos in the left bar.
  4. Click the first photo of a batch, scroll down, hold Shift and click the last one.
  5. Look at the bottom-left corner of the window. It says how many items are selected. Write that number down.
  6. Right-click the selection and choose Always keep on this device. Wait until every file shows a green tick. That means the full-quality original has arrived.
  7. Copy the selection into To sort on your drive and wait for the copy to finish.

Photos only on the iPhone, not in iCloud? On the phone go to Settings → Photos → Transfer to Mac or PC → Keep Originals. Plug it in, tap Trust, open the Windows Photos app, choose Import → From a connected device and save to To sort.

I'm on a Mac, so I haven't done the Windows route myself. If a button has a different name on your PC, use the prompt below and Claude will walk you through it.

  1. With Windows: plug the phone in and choose File transfer on the phone. In File Explorer open the phone, then Internal storage → DCIM → Camera, and copy to To sort.
  2. With a Mac: Macs can't open Android phones on their own. Use a free file-transfer app such as OpenMTP, then copy the same Camera folder.
  3. From Google Photos: go to takeout.google.com, select only Google Photos and export. Unzip everything into To sort.

No laptop, or you just want to sort from your phone.

  1. Plug the flash drive into your phone.
  2. iPhone with USB-C: in Photos, select what you want, tap Share, choose Export Unmodified Original and save it to the drive in the Files app.
  3. Older iPhone: use the app that comes with the drive (SanDisk's is called iXpand Drive) and follow its backup button.
  4. Android: open the Files app, go to DCIM → Camera, select, and copy to the drive.
  5. You can make folders right there in the Files app and sort by hand.

Claude can't sort files on a phone. For the automatic sorting in the next steps, plug the same flash drive into a computer.

Leaving it overnight? Keep the laptop awake 🌙

If the laptop falls asleep, the export stops. Before you go to bed:

  • Plug in the charger and leave the lid open.
  • Mac: open System Settings → Battery → Options and turn on Prevent automatic sleeping on power adapter when the display is off.
  • Windows: open Settings → System → Power & battery → Screen and sleep and set When plugged in, put my device to sleep after to Never.
  • Put the drive somewhere flat where the cable can't get knocked.
